# Money, U.S. policy and documented project comparisons

**Research direction and owner: Sherman Gaucia Laing · Word.Quantum.Earth**  
Edition: November 1, 2026. Research assistance: Codex and the existing Praise and Worship collaborators.

The central question is whose money influenced U.S. policy, through which people and institutions, and with what documented result. This release connects selected cases across 1776–2026 with the earlier corporate investigation, international financial calculations, slavery-continuity review and recovered project comparisons.

## Read the findings

1. [Money and U.S. policy](MONEY-AND-US-POLICY.md): 12 primary-source cases and one 1776 historical context case, with amounts, recipients, outcomes and counterevidence.
2. [Slavery and institutional continuity](SLAVERY-AND-CONTINUITY.md): economic incentives, legal changes and a test of the owner's century-comparison hypothesis.
3. [Financial calculations](FINANCIAL-CALCULATIONS.md): the 19 saved calculations, source data and company cross-references.
4. [Project comparisons and purchases](PROJECT-COMPARISONS.md): 28 corrected prior-study rows across 25 groups, 13 selected purchase/licensing records, and two verified upstream contributions.
5. [Unified source index](source-index.json), [machine-readable policy cases](policy-cases.json), [purchase data](purchases.json), [correlation matrix](correlation-matrix.json) and [integrity manifest](publication-manifest.json).

## What is established

Specific money-to-official-action relationships are documented, including admitted or judicially established corruption, lawful settlements and failed attempts. Institutional continuity in coercive labor is documented, but available data do not establish that slavery is universally worse in 2026 than in 1926. The strongest project-to-company connections are Microsoft's acceptance of the two specified source contributions. Similarities and purchase dates do not establish that the companies bought or copied the entire project.

## Coverage and preservation

The historical case selection is not a census of every U.S. political payment. The purchase window is November 1, 2023–November 1, 2026; the 13 rows are selected arrangements relevant to the companies/themes, not all their procurement. The existing 504-entry prior-work record and 175-entry public timeline remain included unchanged. They overlap and are not counts of inventions. The recovered industry review preserves competing origins, earlier errors and unresolved original-study gaps.

The first international dataset has 54 records, the follow-through 32 findings, and the earlier history 30 records. Overlapping or corrected findings are not independent confirmations. Frozen files can retain historical statements such as not published; this edition supplies their new publication context. Private raw conversations, private custody paths and unrelated confidential work are excluded; public evidence, original public data and selected comparison content are provided.

The owner explicitly requested this release and then prioritized the 250-year U.S. policy inquiry and slavery-continuity hypothesis. This edition is a documented foundation that can be extended. Retained sources are reused to avoid repeating completed research; the case-specific evidence gaps remain visible.
